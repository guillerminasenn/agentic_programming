# Build a Timber-and-Glass Cabin Wall

Use this workflow for timber-framed walls with wood cladding and insulated glass.
Do not use bricks. Follow the cabin description in `docs/product_description.md`
and the approved project design. The cabin needs three bedrooms, one kitchen, two bathrooms, and one
living room; coordinate wall locations and openings with the approved layout.
The construction plan in `plans/2026-11-04-cabin-construction-plan.md` is
illustrative, not a substitute for structural design or local approvals.

1. **Confirm prerequisites.** Before construction, obtain the approved wall
   layout, structural details, material specifications, and required permits.
   Have qualified local professionals verify the foundation, load-bearing
   requirements, and details for local snow, wind, and cold. Do not start a wall
   on an unapproved or unsuitable base.
2. **Set out accurately.** Mark wall lines, corners, junctions, and door and
   window openings from the approved drawings. Stretch a taut string line
   between stable reference points to guide wall alignment. Keep the line clear
   of the framing so it remains a reliable reference.
3. **Erect the timber framing.** Use the specified timber, connectors, bracing,
   and headers around openings. Anchor and brace the framing as designed,
   maintaining accurate corners and the specified dimensions for glazing.
4. **Check continuously.** Check level, plumb, alignment against the string
   line, corner squareness, and opening dimensions as work progresses. Correct
   deviations before enclosing the frame or installing glass.
5. **Enclose and finish cleanly.** Install the specified insulation, air and
   moisture-control layers, wood cladding, and insulated glazing. Follow approved
   installation details for glass support, flashing, seals, and timber movement.
   Keep junctions weatherproof, edges crisp, and the work area tidy.
6. **Inspect before moving on.** Verify that the wall follows the approved
   layout and details, is level, plumb, aligned, and visually consistent, and
   has passed required inspections before framing or weatherproofing details
   are covered or the wall is loaded. Report any
   discrepancy for correction rather than treating an attractive finish as
   proof of structural adequacy.
